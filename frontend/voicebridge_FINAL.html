A support tool, not a replacement for a professional interpreter

VoiceBridge is a student prototype. It can mishear words, so both people should check what was understood. In an emergency, call 999. This project is a prototype and is not medical advice.

Your voice is not kept. If you choose "Use your own voice", the copy of your voice is temporary: it's deleted as soon as the appointment ends. Conversations and recordings aren't saved either.

How it works

What happens when you press the button

VoiceBridge uses large AI models for listening, translating and speaking. Most of our work is the layer around them that makes them safe for a GP appointment.

Typical turn, end to end4–5 s
Translation step~3 s
Speaking step~1 s

1. Audio check

Any recording (laptop, iPhone, Android) is converted to one clean format. Empty, too short, too long, silent or very noisy recordings are refused with a clear message before any AI is used.

2. Listen and translate

One Gemini call writes down what was said, detects the language, translates it, and lists urgent symptoms and doubtful words. It fills a fixed form, so the app always gets the same fields back.

3. Speak

ElevenLabs reads the translation aloud in a different voice for each direction. If it fails, a backup voice takes over; after 15 seconds the app shows the text only rather than freezing.

4. Remember the conversation

The last six turns go with each new one, so "it started two days ago" is understood. In Urdu this fixed verb gender in 3 of 3 tests (cough is feminine).

What we add on top of the AI

GP interpreter rules: keep negations ("no chest pain" never becomes "chest pain"), keep doses and durations exact, never soften or exaggerate, never add advice. Medical glossary: 106 terms from 40 mock UK GP consultations (PriMock57), each to be checked against the NHS SNOMED CT browser. Fair testing: 17 consultations were kept aside and never used to build anything, and every result is compared with plain Gemini.

Safety and privacy

Built to fail safely

Health conversations leave no room for confident guesses. These protections come from problems we found while testing.

The AI invented symptoms in a noisy room, so we stopped it

With heavy background noise, the AI wrote "I have very sharp chest pain" for a recording about pollution. We added a noise check that measures speech against background before any AI runs, and a rule to answer "inaudible" instead of guessing.

Before
Invented chest pain in 2 of 2 noisy tests
After
"Inaudible", no invented symptoms, 4 of 4

Urgent alerts that stay visible

Red flags stay pinned for the GP until dismissed. A doctor asking "do you have chest pain?" or a patient saying "no fever" doesn't raise an alert.

See the original words

Each message shows what was heard in the speaker's own language, with doubtful words underlined and a "Not what I said" button to repeat it.

Nothing is kept

Appointments live only in the server's memory and are deleted when they end, or after two hours. No accounts, no recordings saved. Optional voice copies (only with consent) are deleted when the appointment ends.

Access code and limits

A clinic access code, 20 turns per appointment and a rate limit stop strangers using up the service.

Known limits

It can mishear similar words. In one Urdu test, "released" was heard as "burnt to death", with full confidence. This is why the original words are always shown.

Translations are checked by AI, not yet by native speakers. Native-speaker checks are part of our testing plan.

Audio is sent to Google and ElevenLabs. Fine for a prototype with test data; real patient data would need an NHS-approved data agreement.

It supports a conversation; it doesn't replace a professional interpreter. This project is a prototype and is not medical advice.

Then press record and talk for about 20 seconds, in any language. You could read this:

"Hello. I'm recording a short sample so VoiceBridge can learn how my voice sounds. I'm speaking calmly and clearly, at my normal speed, in a quiet room."
